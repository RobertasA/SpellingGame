# Changelog

## Little Star Witch 2.4

**No level with too many hard keys**
- A level's phonics still decide which words it can use; the keys now decide which of those
  words are used today. Besides a keyboard level's own new letters, the words of a level lean on
  at most two other keys that are not yet known (three in the phonics levels from level 11).
  Words that would need any further unknown key wait for another day.
- The few extra keys let in are the ones that open up the most of the level's words, with the
  keys being worked on in key practice preferred. As keys become known, more of the level's
  words come into play, so playing a level again brings different words.
- If too few of a level's words fit (a child who starts far into the game with no keys known
  yet), the level is filled with the words that need the fewest extra keys.
- Words that come back from earlier levels, and the words topped up for brewing, follow the
  same rule.

## Little Star Witch 2.3.1

**Updates arrive whole**
- After an update the game could open as a mix of new and old files: the new title page with the
  old game behind it, so a new button did nothing and the version number stayed the same. A new
  version is now fetched fresh in one go, and if it arrives while the title page is showing the
  page reloads itself. It never reloads in the middle of a game.

## Little Star Witch 2.3

**Learning the keyboard**
- **⌨️ Keys** on the title page is a short key practice: ten stars with one letter each. No
  brewing afterwards; it is a warm-up for the fingers.
- The letters are chosen by spaced repetition. At most three keys are worked on at a time, and
  they come round most often. A key found quickly and without help a few times counts as known;
  then the next key joins. Known keys come back less and less often, and a key that goes wrong,
  hides or turns slow comes back sooner. "Quick" is judged against the child's own usual time.
- Until every key has been met in the levels, new keys join in the order the levels teach them.
  After that the most common letters in English go first:
  `e t a o i n s h r d l c u m w f g y p b v k j x q z`.
- Typing in the ordinary levels counts too, and the words of a level that use a key being
  worked on come up a little sooner.
- **A picture of the keyboard**: when a letter can't be found on a real keyboard, a small
  keyboard appears at the bottom of the screen with that key lit (the screen keyboard already
  glows its own key). In key practice a brand-new key is pointed out straight away. It follows
  the "Glow the next letter when stuck" setting.

**Keys on the grown-ups page**
- The letters in order of how common they are in English, marked as known, being practised,
  waiting or not met yet.
- Which keys your child takes longest to find: a little keyboard coloured green, yellow or red
  against their own usual speed, with the typical time under each key and a count of wrong keys
  pressed when that one was wanted. Below it, the slowest keys and the most common mix-ups (for
  example `b` for `d`).
- Each key is timed from the letter before it in the same word, so the first letter of a word
  (which also takes reading or remembering) is never timed, and neither is anything across a
  pause, a peek or a star hiding. In key practice a letter is timed from when its star pops out.
  Typing counts in flying and in brewing; tapping sound tiles does not. Only the last 20 times
  for each key count, so the picture follows the child.
- *Clear key timings and practice* starts the keys afresh without touching stars; "Start again
  from the beginning" clears them too.

**Keyboard**
- Holding a key down on a real keyboard now counts as one press, not a stream of letters.

## Little Star Witch 2.2.1

**Sound**
- A line being said is no longer cut off by the next one. Lines wait their turn (with a short
  breath between them); a passing remark that has waited too long is dropped instead of arriving
  late. Only something the player asks for (tapping a word, "hear it again", the next story
  page) interrupts.
- While anything is being said the music drops to a murmur and the sound effects step back, and
  both return a moment after the last line. Before, the music only dipped a little, and only
  for recordings.
- Home recordings are brought up to one speaking level when played, with a limiter, a rumble
  filter and a little extra clarity on consonants, so a quiet take is as easy to hear as a loud
  one. Recordings do not need to be made again.
- New recordings keep more of the quiet edges of a word, so a soft "s" or "f" is not trimmed.

## Little Star Witch 2.2

**Keys first, phonics second**
- The levels are in a new order. The first ten are about finding the keys: each brings in two to
  four new letters (`s a t p`, then `i n`, `m d`, `g o`, `c k`, `e u`, `r h`, `b f l`, `j v w`,
  `x y z q`) and uses only words spelt one letter per sound with letters already met. The banner
  at the start of a level names the new keys.
- Phonics starts at level 11 with the gentlest "two letters, one sound" spellings
  (`ck ff ll ss zz`), then `sh ch`, `th ng` and onwards. Nothing was dropped: the 24 phonics and
  spelling groups now share 20 levels, so a few later levels cover more.
- The whole of the Cottage Garden shows one star at a time, and the keyboard levels give longer
  to type each word.
- Brewing in the keyboard levels uses words of four letters or fewer, and the star challenge
  (hear it, then spell it) only asks for a two- or three-letter word.
- Saved stars stay with their level number, so after this update a star may sit on a level whose
  words have changed. "Start again from the beginning" on the grown-ups page clears them.

**Keyboard**
- The on-screen keyboard stays out of the way while a real keyboard is being typed on, in the
  star challenge as well as when flying. Touching the screen while spelling brings it back.
  When the challenge keyboard is hidden, a stuck child sees the next letter faintly in its place.

## Little Star Witch 2.1

**Things to find**
- Eighteen woodland visitors (three per place, one of them shy) pop out of hiding places in the
  middle of a level and hop along. The map shows who has been found and who is still hiding;
  a couple of old friends tag along at the start of every level.
- Every level of a place is flown at a different time of day, with its scenery laid out afresh.

**Reading and spelling**
- Words to type no longer have a picture beside them, so they have to be read. The picture pops
  out as the reward once the word is spelled.
- Brewing ends with a star challenge: the word is heard and pictured but never shown, and is
  written letter by letter on a whole keyboard. Grown-ups can choose "always look first" or
  "challenges only".

**Voice**
- The voice-over can be turned off (🗣️ on the title screen, the pause card and while playing) so
  the child does the reading.
- Your own recordings replace the computer voice. The Voice studio page records every word and
  sentence in the browser, trims the silence, and exports or imports a zip.

**Looks**
- New renderer: soft shadows, glow, filmic colour, textured fur / wood / stone / cloth, swaying
  leaves and grass, rippling water, instanced ground cover, drifting fireflies, snow and leaves.
- Three graphics levels, stepping down by itself on slower devices.

**Not yet tried on real hardware**: frame rate on tablets, real speech voices, and recording with a
real microphone in Safari and Firefox were not available where this was tested.
